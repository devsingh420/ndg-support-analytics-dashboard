Business Case & Context

Why the numbers above look the way they do — framed for a client conversation, not just a data dump.

Declining Ticket Volume Is a Normal Post-Launch Pattern

This mirrors what's typically seen in enterprise ecommerce rollouts — often called a "hypercare" period: a short window of concentrated support demand immediately after go-live, followed by rapid stabilization as both the platform and the team operating it settle in. A sharp early peak followed by a steep drop-off is the expected shape of a healthy launch, not a sign of an unstable platform.

Payment Issues Are Inherent to This Kind of Ecommerce — Not a Quality Failure

This is not a simple storefront running flat-rate, one-time purchases. It integrates Omise for payment processing alongside subscription billing, promotional/bundle pricing, and capsule-credit/loyalty logic layered on top of every transaction. Each of those is a distinct way a payment can behave unexpectedly — complexity a plain checkout simply wouldn't have. If this were flat-out purchases with no subscriptions and no promotions, payment issues would be far easier to avoid entirely.

Order volume and system uptime figures on this tab are reported directly by the team, not derived from the Roomie ticket log — flagged with a "Reported" badge above to keep the provenance clear, consistent with how every other number on this dashboard is sourced.

Fixes Are Fast — Release Timing Follows UAT and the Release Cycle

PNG

The team's own fix turnaround (workbook Duration, active hours) is consistently fast across every severity. The gap to total elapsed time is queue time plus client UAT/testing plus waiting for the next scheduled production release — not slower development.
